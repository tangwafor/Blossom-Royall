# My Fit Signature Product

## Product promise

My Fit turns one private measurement passport into explainable recommendations across clothing, rings, bracelets, and necklaces. It never presents an estimate as certainty. Each recommendation shows the measurement method, confidence, seller sizing system, onsite availability, online availability, and the safest next action.

## Research foundation

1. ISO 8653 defines ring sizing through calibrated ring measurement and identifies a finger gauge set as the jeweler to consumer method. Ring size is represented through the inner circumference in millimeters. Source: https://www.iso.org/standard/65408.html

2. GIA recommends calibrated narrow or wide finger gauges, requires the fit to pass the joint and settle correctly, and treats bands wider than 4 millimeters differently. GIA also warns that paper and string estimates can be inaccurate. Source: https://www.gia.edu/quality-assurance-benchmark/accurate-determination-finger-ring-size

3. Cartier publishes international conversion tables based on finger circumference in millimeters and advises measuring at different times and on different days. Source: https://www.cartier.com/on/demandware.static/-/Library-Sites-CartierSharedLibrary-BGTJ/default/dwc1c9f9e6/size-guide-pdf/rings-size-table-data.pdf

4. Tiffany requires printable guides to be printed at full scale and calibrated with a known physical object. Its necklace guidance distinguishes neck circumference, chain length, pendant shape, and layering position. Sources: https://media.tiffany.com/is/content/Tiffany/Tiffany_Ring_Size_Guide_A4-EU/ and https://www.tiffany.com/client-services/size-guide/necklaces-pendants-size.html

5. ASTM maintains separate terminology and measurement tables for different apparel populations. This supports using body dimensions as an input while matching against each seller specific garment chart instead of pretending one universal numerical size exists. Source: https://www.astm.org/get-involved/technical-committees/committee-d13/subcommittee-d13/jurisdiction-d1355

6. The FTC warns that body related and biometric information can create privacy, security, bias, and accuracy risks. Claims about AI accuracy must be substantiated, collection must be transparent, and meaningful user choice is essential. Source: https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2023/05/ftc-warns-about-misuses-biometric-information-harm-consumers

## Experience model

1. Measure with a calm visual guide.

2. Verify important measurements twice.

3. Label the method and confidence.

4. Translate the private passport into the exact seller chart for the selected item.

5. Show whether the matched variant is available onsite, orderable online, available by special order, or unavailable.

6. Explain why the item matches and what could change the recommendation.

7. Offer an in store verification appointment for valuable jewelry, custom work, uncertain measurements, and high return risk.

## Confidence levels

1. Verified in store: measured by authorized staff with a calibrated tool.

2. Verified at home: approved method completed twice within tolerance.

3. Estimated: usable for browsing, but seller confirmation is required before final purchase.

4. Incomplete: not used for automatic recommendations.

## Release boundaries

Automatic photo measurement, seller chart ingestion, exact variant matching, printable scale calibration, appointment booking, and recommendation learning are still in development. They must not be represented as production ready until backend authorization, privacy, accuracy, accessibility, device, and real account tests pass.
